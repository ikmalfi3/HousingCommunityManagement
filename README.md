# Housing Community Management

Offline Windows Forms application with SQLite storage. Houses exist independently of owners. Create a numeric range with an optional prefix and zero-padding (for example A-001 through A-100); existing numbers are skipped. Each house supports an owned/unowned status, multiple owner names and contact numbers, multiple flags, and an active/inactive record status. House numbers are unique ignoring ASCII letter case.

Enter the maintenance amount per house per month in **Charge houses** for each charging run. **Community settings** manages flags and accounting. Owners and contacts are entered one per line. In the house editor, type a flag name above the checkboxes and click **Add** (or press Enter). Adding a flag immediately remembers its name for all houses and checks it for the current house; click Save to apply the selection to that house. Reopening the editor lists remembered names and all flags used on other houses. Flag names are trimmed and duplicates are matched without case. There are no predefined manual flags; unused original starter suggestions are removed on opening an older database. Flags store their source; billing and renovations now calculate automatic flags whenever households are read. Derived flags are read-only in the editor, and manual edits preserve them. Unowned houses can be saved without owners or contacts. Ownership, occupancy flags, and active status are independent.

## Run

The app opens to `MainForm`, a dashboard with sidebar navigation to Dashboard, Households, Billing & payments, Renovations, Income & expenditure, Accounting and Settings. Household management and billing open inside the main window. Returning to Dashboard refreshes house counts, current-month collection and outstanding amounts, all-month overdue amounts and recent payments. All forms have Designer-editable layouts.

## Monthly charges and flexible collection

Billing & payments has two tabs: **Charges & payments** retains the monthly filter, charging dialog and payment recording; **Summary** has an independent year spinner and one row per house (including inactive houses), owners, contacts and January-December columns. House details stay frozen while scrolling horizontally. Month cells show remaining balances, or paid totals for settled charges. Blue means charged but not overdue, red means a remaining balance after its saved deadline, green means fully paid, and grey means no charge. Partial payments retain the outstanding status until fully settled. Tooltips show charged, paid, remaining and due-date details. The summary refreshes when opened, when its year changes, or when Refresh is clicked.

Open **Billing & payments** and choose the year and months to display. The **All** checkbox selects/deselects all months and shows an indeterminate state for a partial selection. With no months selected, no charge records are shown. The current month is selected on opening.

Click **Charge houses...** to open a separate dialog. Its year and month checkboxes are independent of the table filters, with their own **All** checkbox. Enter a positive **Amount per house per month (RM)**, then select houses individually from the active-house listing or use **Select all houses**. The amount starts at RM 0 and must be entered for each run. **Charge date**, beside the amount, defaults to today and applies to all new charges and their accounting entries in that run. Historical dates are accepted from 1900 through today, but cannot precede the accounting opening date once accounting has started. Existing charges keep their original dates and amounts. Older charges without a recorded charge date retain an unknown date; existing journals are preserved. **Only show owned houses** filters the listing; hidden unowned selections are cleared, and Select all applies only to visible houses. The selected count is shown below the list. At least one month and one house must be selected. Cancelling creates no charges.

Click **Charge selected months** in the dialog to create one charge per selected month and selected house at the amount entered in that dialog. Previously charged house/year/month combinations are skipped. Unknown or inactive house selections are rejected. The popup supports one shared due date or independently editable dates for each selected month.

By default each month is due on its own 6th. Check **Use multi-month due dates** to show a separate date spinner for each selected month, initially set to that month's 6th in the selected year. Edit the day, month or year with the spinner. Unchecking and reselecting a month, or temporarily switching due-date modes, preserves its edits; changing the billing year moves the charge date, shared due date and individual due dates into that year while preserving their months and days (29 February becomes 28 February in a non-leap year). The multi-month and shared-date options are mutually exclusive. For grouped collection, check **Use one due date** and choose a shared date before generating charges (for example, select January-March with a shared 6 January deadline). Any unpaid balance becomes overdue the following day. Generation never changes existing charges or their deadlines, and later charging amounts only affect newly generated charges.

Select a monthly charge and click **Record payment** to enter a full or partial payment, date and reference. Payments reduce that month's balance; overpayments are rejected. Use **Record multiple payments...** to allocate one receipt across several months or record payments from several houses in one batch, with one row per monthly charge. No monthly-payer or quarterly-payer categories are needed.

**Record multiple payments...** opens an unpaid-charge table using the billing page's selected year/months. Choose a shared payment date and **Received into** cash/bank account. Filter by year (including All years), months or house/owner text. Each row has a selection checkbox, house, month, due date, remaining balance, editable payment amount and reference. Amounts initially equal the remaining balance and can be reduced for partial payments. Settled charges are excluded; unpaid charges on inactive houses remain payable.

Use **Select all visible unpaid charges**, **Clear selection**, or **Fill selected with remaining balance**. The footer shows selected count and total; invalid amounts disable saving. Filter changes clear selections that become hidden, while retaining entered amounts/references for later review. **Refresh unpaid charges** reloads current balances and clears draft entries after confirmation when edits exist. Cancelling the dialog saves nothing. Different dates or receiving accounts should be recorded in separate batches.

Click **Record payments** and confirm the count, total, payment date and receiving account. Every selected row becomes a separate maintenance payment against its own month. With accounting started, each payment produces a balanced entry into the selected cash/bank account and the original receivable account, including charges carried into opening balances or created under older defaults. The complete batch saves in one database transaction. Missing charges, changed balances, overpayments, duplicate charge rows, invalid dates/accounts/references or a failed journal write reject the entire batch. Refresh and review if another payment changed a displayed balance. The original single-payment action remains available.
Existing quarterly charges are converted transactionally into three monthly charges on first opening. Their total amounts and original due dates are preserved. Any indivisible sen are assigned to the earlier months. Old payments are allocated oldest month first, preserving payment dates, references and original payment IDs; original quarterly tables remain as an archive. Migration runs once and migrated months cannot be charged again.

Financial status is calculated independently of manually assigned house flags. Dashboard collection means payments allocated to the current month (not all cash received during that month). Overdue balance covers all generated months past their due dates. Automatic house flags and expense claims/reversals are available. Maintenance payment reversals, credits and financial reports are not implemented yet.

To delete a flag option, select it in the house editor and click **Delete**. Deletion is immediate and allowed when the flag is unused or its only use is on the currently opened house. The flag is removed from that house and the options together, even if you subsequently cancel other edits. If another house uses it (including inactive houses and derived flags), deletion is blocked until those uses are removed and saved.

Open `HousingCommunityManagement.sln` in Visual Studio with the .NET desktop development workload and .NET 9 SDK. Set `HousingCommunityManagement` as the startup project and press F5. NuGet restores Entity Framework Core SQLite automatically; no SQLite server or manual database setup is needed.

The database is created at `Data/community.db` beside the executable. Run the application from a writable folder. Debug and Release output folders have separate databases. Keep the database with the app when moving it, and back it up with the app closed. Automatic backup and restore are not implemented yet.

## Structure

Drag the houses table's column-header dividers to resize columns. Widths are saved on closing the main window to `Data/house-table.json` beside the database and restored on the next run. Owners and contacts appear one per line, wrap within their columns, and automatically increase row height to display all entries.

- `HousingCommunityManagement`: WinForms screens.
- `HousingCommunityManagement.Core`: household model, validation and repository interface.
- `HousingCommunityManagement.Data`: EF Core SQLite persistence.
- `HousingCommunityManagement.Checks`: integration checks using a temporary database.

Run checks with `dotnet run --project HousingCommunityManagement.Checks`.

The database is created on first use. The original single-owner schema is upgraded transactionally on opening: house IDs, active status, owner and contact details are preserved, and nonempty owners imply owned status. The old global fee value is retained internally for database compatibility and is no longer used to generate charges. Original amounts remain in the `LegacyHouseholdFees` table for reference. Existing databases gain the monthly charge and payment tables on opening. Future schema changes need explicit migrations.

All forms keep their controls in `.Designer.cs` files for visual editing in Visual Studio. The checks cover range generation and overlaps, ownership, multiple owners and contacts, flag source persistence, community settings, validation and upgrades from the original database.

## Renovations

Open **Renovations** in the sidebar and choose **New renovation...**. Select an active, owned house, enter a description, start date and planned duration in calendar days. The start date counts as day one. Enter a one-off deposit amount chosen by the user (RM 0 if no deposit is required); duration does not calculate the deposit.

Select a record and an action date to **Collect deposit**, **Mark completed**, or **Return deposit**. Positive deposits must be collected before completion. Completed renovations retain an **Awaiting return** status until the actual full refund is recorded. Collection and return can each be recorded only once; future dates and returns before completion or archiving are rejected. These actions record money received or returned outside the app; they do not transfer funds. Renovation deposits are stored separately from maintenance payments. The page shows planned, in-progress, overdue and completed work, deposit dates, amounts held and refunds awaiting return. Existing databases gain the renovation table automatically.

Use the Renovations filters for renovation status, deposit status and start year; All options remove each filter. Filters combine and the summary reflects the visible rows. Start year means the year the renovation begins, including work spanning years. Select a row and click **Delete renovation** to permanently remove it if no deposit was collected. Once money has been collected, the action becomes **Archive renovation** and preserves all deposit and accounting history. Choose **Include archived**, status **Archived**, or deposit status **Awaiting return** to find archived records. An archived held deposit can be returned without marking the renovation completed. Archiving does not return money to the owner. Filters stay selected after refresh, actions and deletion.

## Accounting setup and posting

Open **Settings > Accounting > Open accounting settings...** for the chart of accounts, defaults and opening balances. The **Chart of accounts** tab provides editable account codes, names, types, cash/bank designation and active status. Select a row to edit it or click **New account**. There is no account deletion action. Account codes are unique ignoring case. Used account types and cash/bank designations are locked. Before deactivating a default account, select and save a replacement under **Default accounts**.

Defaults cover maintenance receivable, maintenance income, refundable renovation deposits, receipts, refunds, opening community funds and accounts payable. These mappings are checked for the appropriate account type. Receipts/refunds must use active asset accounts marked as cash/bank. Defaults affect new transactions only: payments and refunds settle the receivable or liability account originally used, even when that default has subsequently changed.

Accounting is initially **not started** so an existing database is not silently assigned historical bank balances. Save the initial chart of accounts and review defaults. On **Chart of accounts**, use the highlighted **Next: start accounting** area below the account list and click **Start accounting...**. The dialog lets you choose an opening date (1900 through today) above the active cash/bank account list and enter balances brought forward into that date, including held renovation deposit money. Cancelling saves no opening balances. This creates opening cash/bank entries, each outstanding maintenance balance as a receivable, and each unreturned renovation deposit as a liability, including archived deposits, balanced against opening community funds. Income and payments before the opening date are not reposted. Accounting starts on the selected opening date, once only; the opening date and balances are locked after confirmation. Opening journals use that date. Opening balances represent the position immediately before activity on the opening date. Maintenance receivables subtract only payments before that date. Held deposits include collections before opening that were unreturned at that point, including deposits refunded later. Existing charges, maintenance receipts, deposit collections and refunds on or after opening are posted once at their original dates, using their saved cash/bank accounts where available (otherwise the configured defaults). Charges without a recorded charge date are treated as brought forward. Enter cash/bank opening balances before opening-day activity so these transactions are not counted twice. Verify them before starting. New cash/bank accounts added after starting begin with zero ledger balance; noncash adjustments are available under Accounting > Adjustments; cash/bank transfers can be recorded through Money in / out.

After starting, generating maintenance charges automatically debits receivable and credits maintenance income, dated on the selected charge date. **Record payment** debits the selected **Received into** account and credits the original receivable. Renovations has **Receive into** and **Refund from** selectors. Collection debits cash/bank and credits refundable deposits; return debits the original deposit liability and credits the selected cash/bank account. Completion and archiving create no accounting entries. Receipts and refunds cannot be dated before accounting started. These actions record actual money movements outside the app; they do not transfer money.

Every source transaction and its balanced journal entry save in one database transaction. Duplicate source postings are prevented. The **Accounting** sidebar page has Journal, Account balances, Registers, Adjustments, Reconciliation, and Locks & history tabs. **Journal** shows immutable posted entries and their source references; **Account balances** shows debit/credit totals and balances (assets/expenses use debit minus credit; liabilities/equity/income use credit minus debit). Account balances covers the full posted ledger; Journal initially shows all dates. Both refresh when the page opens, when its tab changes, or when Refresh is clicked. Account setup and opening balances remain in Settings; the journal and balance views are no longer in that dialog. These are an initial ledger, not a full set of financial statements. Expense recording and expense payment reversals/cancellations are available as described below. Noncash manual journals, asset/supplier-deposit/prepayment register actions, bank reconciliation and optional transaction locks are available. Dedicated maintenance/renovation corrections and financial statements remain separate enhancements; there is no mandatory year-close operation. Before accounting starts, new payment/deposit records retain their selected cash/bank account but do not create journal entries.

The checks cover setup and validation, opening balances, cash/bank selection, posting after changed defaults, archived refunds, source/journal rollback on failure and reconciliation of receivables and deposit liabilities.

## Income & expenditure

Use **Income & expenditure** in the sidebar for **Supplier bill**, **Reimbursement claim** or **Direct expense** records. **Payees & categories...** manages reusable suppliers, claimants, contacts and expense categories. The same setup is accessible from **Settings > Accounting > Default accounts > Payees & expense categories...**. Each category maps to an active expense account. A supplier bill requires a supplier; a claim requires a claimant; direct expenses can use either. Existing payees/categories can be deactivated rather than deleted. The accounts payable default is created automatically when upgrading an existing database, without changing existing journal entries or opening balances.

Create **New expense...**, choose its type and payee, document date, accounting date and optional due date, then enter the invoice/receipt reference and description. Add one or more lines with a category, optional line description and positive amount. Dates for documents/accounting cannot be in the future. References must be unique for each payee among uncancelled records, ignoring case, when not blank. Draft edits use a version check to prevent overwriting newer changes.

Attach PDF, PNG, JPEG or text receipts (up to 10 files, each up to 10 MB and at most 30 MB in total). Attachments are stored inside the SQLite database, so they remain with its backup. The main page's **Attachments** tab can save a selected file to disk. Attachments are edited with drafts and preserved after posting or cancellation.

**Save draft** creates no ledger entry. Select a draft and choose **Post / approve** to debit its expense accounts and credit the saved accounts payable default. The accounting date must be on or after accounting started; start accounting in Settings before posting. Posting freezes bill details, expense accounts and payee name. Category/default changes affect drafts and new bills, not posted entries. This is a local confirmation step, not a separate multi-user approval workflow.

**Record payment...** supports full or partial amounts with a payment date, reference and active cash/bank **Paid from** account. It debits the original payable account and credits cash/bank, without recording the expense twice. Overpayments and payments dated before posting/accounting start are rejected. **Save, post & record payment** in the editor records the entire amount paid immediately, atomically with posting; use this for direct expenses or already-paid bills/claims. Payment fields at the bottom of the editor apply only to this combined action. These actions record payments made outside the app and do not execute bank transfers.

Status is **Draft**, **Unpaid**, **Partially paid**, **Paid** or **Cancelled**. Overdue is calculated separately when an unpaid posted balance is past its due date. Filters cover status, type, document year and overdue-only; summary totals follow the filtered rows. Select a record to see its expense lines, payment history and attachments.

Only drafts can be permanently deleted. For a mistaken payment, select it under **Payment history** and click **Reverse selected payment...**, enter a date and reason, and confirm. This adds an opposite journal entry using the original accounts and reopens the bill balance. Record a reversal only for an incorrect entry or after money has actually been returned; it does not recover money from the recipient. Duplicate reversals are blocked. A posted bill can be **Cancelled** once all payments have been reversed; cancellation requires a reason and reverses the original bill entry, preserving the bill, attachments and payment history. The cancellation reason is in the reversal journal and the expense-lines tab tooltip. Cancellation/reversal dates cannot precede the entries being corrected; new payments cannot precede earlier reversal dates. Re-enter a corrected bill as a new draft; a cancelled reference can be reused.

The integration checks cover expense drafts and versions, duplicate references, attachment persistence, multi-line posting, partial payments, claim/direct payments, changed account/category mappings, reversals, cancellation, reconciliation of accounts payable and rollback of the entire source/ledger operation on failure. Recurring bills are not implemented.

## Delete/reset trial data

At the very bottom of **Settings**, choose **Choose data to delete...**. Select categories individually or use **Select all data**. Nothing is selected initially. The selector shows current record counts, includes archived renovations, and explains linked deletions. Households include their maintenance charges/payments and renovations. Suppliers/claimants or expense-category resets include expenses, payments and attachments. Resetting accounting includes all financial modules and expense categories so account references cannot be left broken.

Click **Delete selected data...** to review the exact selected categories and complete a fresh six-character code containing lowercase letters and numbers. The final **Delete permanently** button stays disabled until the code matches exactly; uppercase, missing characters and incorrect codes are rejected. Cancelling either dialog deletes nothing. The confirmation can be used only once. Deletion is permanent and takes effect immediately after confirmation, independently of Save settings.

Deleting a financial module also deletes its associated journal entries. Partial resets preserve unselected transactions, account mappings, accounting activation and opening cash/bank balances. An accounting reset instead clears the complete journal/opening balances, restores the default chart and categories, and returns accounting to not-started state. Resetting community settings clears saved flag definitions and assignments. Automatic flags continue to reflect any billing/renovation records that remain. All-data reset leaves a usable empty community with default accounts/categories; it does not remove the database schema. Legacy charge/payment archives are cleared when maintenance is selected, and legacy house fees are cleared when households are selected; migration markers remain so deleted records are not reimported on opening.

The deletion and default restoration run in one database transaction; any failure rolls the entire operation back. On success Settings closes, unsaved settings edits are discarded, open pages are closed, and the dashboard reloads. Layout preferences are retained. Integration checks use temporary databases to cover every category, dependency selection, incorrect/uppercase captcha, complete reset, legacy cleanup, restart and rollback on failure.

## Automatic house flags

Billing and renovations derive these flags for each house, including inactive houses:

| Flag | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| Maintenance fee outstanding | At least one maintenance charge has a positive remaining balance, even if its deadline is in the future. |
| Maintenance fee overdue | An unpaid charge is past its saved due date. The due date itself is not overdue. |
| Upcoming renovation | At least one unfinished, unarchived renovation starts after today. |
| Currently in renovation | At least one unfinished, unarchived renovation has started, including overdue work. |
| Renovation overdue | An unfinished, unarchived renovation is past its inclusive planned end date. |
| Renovation deposit awaiting collection | An unfinished, unarchived renovation has a positive deposit amount that has not been collected. |
| Renovation deposit held | A positive deposit was collected and has not been returned, including completed or archived renovations. |
| Renovation deposit awaiting return | A completed or archived renovation has a collected, positive deposit that has not been returned. |

Multiple records may produce different flags on the same house, but each automatic flag appears only once. Zero-deposit renovations have no deposit flags. Full payment, completion, refund and source-record deletion remove flags as soon as their conditions no longer apply. Billing flags use source `Billing`; renovation flags use source `Renovations`.

These flags are calculated on read, not persisted as snapshots. They appear in the household table, can be searched using its search box, and are displayed one per line under **Derived flags (read only)** in the house editor. They are excluded from the manual checklist, cannot be added as custom flag options or deleted manually, and are not changed by saving unrelated house details. Custom flags and unrelated existing flag sources remain independent. The editor reloads current source information when opened. Returning to Households or clicking **Refresh** reloads current flags; a minute timer also refreshes the household page after the date changes so deadlines and start/end dates do not require a data edit. Settings continues to manage manual flags only.

Integration checks cover all eight rules, due/start/end-date boundaries, multiple concurrent records, partial/full payments, completion, archived refunds, zero deposits, source deletion, preserved custom flags, repeated calculation, manual-edit guards and absence of persisted computed snapshots. UI checks verify read-only display, manual-list separation, searching and new-house construction.

The **Journal** tab supports optional **From** and **To** date bounds (check each box to enable it; both dates are inclusive), an **Account** selector including inactive accounts, **Transaction type**, and case-insensitive **Search** across descriptions, account codes/names, transaction types, entry numbers and source references. Filters combine and apply immediately. **Clear filters** restores all lines. Selections are retained on Refresh and tab switches. A From date after To displays a validation message and no rows. The footer shows matching entry/line counts and filtered debit/credit totals. Account and account-text filters show only matching lines, so filtered debits and credits need not balance; the Account balances tab always uses the full ledger.

The **Dashboard** combines current cash/bank ledger balances, maintenance outstanding across all years, unpaid posted expenses and refundable deposits held (including archived renovations). Its year selector controls the monthly money chart and maintenance collection progress; current balances and attention items remain independent of that selector. Cash/bank displays **Not configured** until accounting starts. The chart separates maintenance receipts, net expense payments, deposits received and deposits returned, with exact amounts on hover. Expense payment reversals reduce net payments in the reversal month.

Attention links open overdue maintenance across all years, overdue expenses, overdue renovations or deposits awaiting return. Other overview links open their respective pages. Changing the billing year/month filters exits the dashboard's all-year view. The lower sections show maintenance progress, unfinished renovations and planned end dates, unpaid expenses and recent payment/deposit activity. Refresh or returning to Dashboard reloads the records. Summary cards wrap into two rows at narrower window sizes.

Historical entry: expenses retain separate document and accounting dates; posting and payments must be on or after accounting opening. Expense payments cannot precede posting, reversals cannot precede payment, and cancellation cannot precede posting or payment reversals. Renovations may have historical start and action dates; completion follows start and collection, and refunds follow collection and completion (or archiving). Collections and refunds posted after accounting starts must be on or after opening. Deposit collection may precede the renovation start, for advance deposits. Existing started ledgers are preserved; this change does not recalculate locked opening entries.

## General money in / out

**Income & expenditure** opens with **Money in / out**, alongside **Bills & payments** for the existing supplier bills, reimbursement claims, direct expenses, partial settlements and draft workflows.

Use **Money in...** or **Money out...** for a direct receipt or payment already made outside the app. Enter its historical date (on or after accounting opening, no later than today), cash/bank account, optional payer/payee, required description, reference and optional related general transaction. Split the positive total across up to 100 account lines. Each line selects the account describing the movement: income, operating expense, equipment, supplier deposits, prepayments, other assets/liabilities/equity or a different cash/bank account. Account codes and names come from Chart of accounts; add the needed asset/income accounts there first. General transactions select accounts directly rather than expense categories. Payer/payee names are saved on the transaction without requiring supplier setup.

Money in debits the selected bank and credits the line accounts; money out credits the bank and debits the line accounts. Examples: an equipment purchase is Money out to an equipment asset account; a utility deposit is Money out to a deposit asset account; its refund is Money in to the same asset account. A bank transfer is one Money out entry from the sending account allocated to the receiving cash/bank account (or the equivalent Money in entry). Record a transfer once. Linked maintenance receivable, renovation deposit liability and bill payable accounts, including historic control accounts, remain reserved for their dedicated workflows so their ledgers reconcile.

Attachments support PDF, PNG, JPEG and text, up to 10 files, 10 MB each and 30 MB total, stored with the transaction. Confirmation posts the transaction and balanced journal together in one database transaction; there is no general-transaction draft or editing after posting. **View transaction...** shows immutable details, splits, attachments and links. **Reverse...** requires a date on/after the original and a reason, posts opposite journal lines once and preserves the original. An actual refund is a new opposite-direction transaction against the appropriate original account, optionally linked to the original; reversal is for corrections. The same nonblank reference, payer/payee, direction and bank cannot be recorded twice while unreversed.

The listing filters by transaction year, direction, status and text (including account names). Its totals cover unreversed displayed records and include transfers; they are not an income statement or an as-of balance. Accounting includes general entries and reversals in the journal and account balances. Dashboard recent activity and yearly cash movement include these entries and reversals; external receipt/payment chart totals exclude cash/bank transfer allocations. The Income & expenditure reset category clears both general transactions and bills and their associated journals.

This extension records asset purchases and supplier deposits in the ledger. Individual asset/deposit/prepayment registers, depreciation, noncash application of supplier advances and bank reconciliation are available under Accounting. Financial statements remain a separate enhancement. Existing expense history and locked opening balances are preserved.

## Full opening balances and corrections

Start accounting still begins with cash/bank balances and a chosen opening date. Negative bank balances represent overdrafts. **Other opening balances...** adds three schedules: other noncash asset/liability/equity debit/credit balances; individual equipment, supplier deposits and prepayments (with original cost and accumulated depreciation where applicable); and supplier/claimant bills still owed. Dates are entered as dd/MM/yyyy or yyyy-MM-dd. Create the needed accounts and suppliers before opening setup. Enter only the amount still owed on opening supplier bills, not the original invoice amount if partly paid before handover.

Supporting register/bill amounts populate their ledger accounts automatically, against opening community funds. Do not enter those totals again under Other balances. Maintenance receivables and renovation deposits continue to come from their original records. **Review all balances...** shows the generated debit/credit totals and the balancing opening funds; reviewing performs a transaction rollback and creates no permanent source records, journals, or audit entries. Starting requires the review and then confirmation, and saves all schedules and journals atomically. Opening supplier bills appear in Bills & payments and can be settled normally without recording a new expense.

For an already-started database, **Accounting > Registers > Opening schedules...** adds missing supporting records/balances at the original opening date with a required correction reason, without replacing original entries. An account already entered as an opening balance cannot be entered again under Other balances: use a noncash adjustment for a correction, or adopt the existing ledger value into its register. **Correct bank opening...** records a signed change against opening funds as a separate audited entry. Registered items have guided Opening cost correction and Opening depreciation correction actions; these use the original opening date and validate the entire register timeline. Locks and finalized bank statements must be reviewed/reopened before backdated financial corrections.

## Assets, supplier deposits and prepayments

**Accounting > Registers** lists individual items, acquisition dates, suppliers, locations/serial information, cost/paid amounts, accumulated depreciation and remaining balances. Filter by type and as-of date; the lower grid shows the selected item's movement history. Edit details changes descriptive information with an audit entry, never accounting amounts or source history. An asset's accumulated-depreciation account can also be configured there before depreciation history exists.

**Add item...** links a paid purchase/deposit/prepayment to an existing unreversed Money out allocation to the same asset account. A split purchase can fund multiple items, but cannot be linked beyond its original account allocation. Alternatively adopt an unassigned opening ledger value: this creates a supporting record without another journal. Opening gross cost and accumulated depreciation must be covered by the corresponding opening ledger entries. **Link purchase / refund...** links later additions or partial refunds to the appropriate item. These links do not record money twice. Reversing a linked cash transaction adds opposite register movements in the same transaction, and is blocked if it would make the timeline invalid. **Balance check...** compares registered costs/accumulated depreciation with ledger balances as of the selected date and exposes unassigned differences.

Guided actions include: applying supplier deposits/prepayments to a posted bill for the same supplier; recognizing prepaid costs as expenses; writing off a deposit/prepayment; recording asset depreciation; and disposing/writing off an asset, clearing both its gross cost and accumulated depreciation. Sale proceeds are recorded separately through Money in. Actions update the register, ledger and supplier bill settlement together without a new cash movement. Depreciation amounts are entered by the user rather than generated from an assumed depreciation policy. A fully depreciated asset remains in the register until disposed. Guided actions appear under Adjustments; their dated reversals restore the linked records as well as reversing the journal.

## Noncash adjustments and transaction locks

**Accounting > Adjustments** supports journal drafts with dated debit/credit lines, a description, reference and attachments. Drafts can be edited with version checks and saved unbalanced; posting requires active accounts, balanced positive totals, an unlocked date on/after opening, and no cash/bank or linked control/register accounts. Use dedicated bill/maintenance/renovation/register actions for those accounts. Posted journals are immutable; Reverse adds a reasoned, dated opposite entry. General journal attachments use the same file limits as other transactions. Original supplier payment history also shows noncash deposit/prepayment applications; reverse these from Accounting > Adjustments.

**Locks & history** optionally prevents all financial postings/reversals through a selected date. Locking and unlocking both require a reason, recorded with UTC timestamps. This is a local review control, not a multi-user permission system. There is no mandatory annual close. Finalized bank reconciliations separately protect matched cash entries and block new backdated cash entries in their statement period.

## Bank reconciliation

Create a statement under **Accounting > Reconciliation** with the bank/cash account, dates and signed opening/closing balances. The first statement opening must agree with the ledger opening at its start (including initial opening-bank corrections). Choose an earlier first statement or correct opening records if it does not. Later statements start the following day after the previous finalized statement and use its closing balance.

Open the statement to import CSV with explicit column mapping, date format, and comma/semicolon/tab separator. Map a signed amount (positive in, negative out), or separate positive credit/debit columns. A mapped preview is required. Remove balance/total rows from the CSV; each imported row is an actual nonzero transaction within the statement dates. Reimporting identical lines skips duplicates; repeated identical rows within one full CSV are preserved by occurrence. Manual bank/cashbook lines are also supported. Incorrect unmatched lines can be removed, with their contents retained in statement history.

Check one or more statement and ledger lines to match equal signed totals. One-to-many and many-to-one matches are allowed; already-cleared ledger entries cannot be matched twice. Matching posts no money. For a genuinely missing fee/receipt, **Record selected bank item...** prefills a new Money in/out form; select its account allocation, post once, then match the resulting ledger entry. **Balances...** corrects statement opening/closing values while the statement is open.

Finalize requires correct statement arithmetic, every statement row matched, matching opening continuity, and a zero reconciliation difference after accounting for uncleared ledger entries. Uncleared ledger items carry into the next statement. Reopen requires a reason and preserves history; later finalized statements must be reopened first. Only an unfinished latest statement that has never been finalized can be deleted. A cash account can use cashbook lines and its counted closing balance through the same review process. CSV statement import is read-only until the mapped lines are confirmed; matching/import never creates ledger transactions automatically.

Accounting reset clears registers, adjustments, settlements, reconciliation matches/statements and lock history with their dependent financial records. Selective module resets are blocked when these supporting records depend on them, rather than leaving orphaned accounting data.

## Designer-first UI development

Each form/user control has a matching .cs and .Designer.cs file, with the designable type first in its source file and a public parameterless constructor. Fixed controls, grid columns, tabs, layout, styling and event wiring belong in InitializeComponent as explicit named fields and assignments. Avoid control factories, object initializers, inferred locals, loops, lambdas, service calls and clock-dependent expressions inside designer initialization. Runtime code binds data, performs validation, and updates data-dependent state; it does not replace the designer-owned page structure. Dashboard summary entries are data-driven, while its fixed sections remain designer-owned.

The project explicitly marks forms/user controls and nests Designer/resource files for Visual Studio. Reopen the solution after this project metadata changes. Run `dotnet run --project HousingCommunityManagement.DesignerChecks` on Windows to check all designer sources and construct/render every screen without application services. Run the existing HousingCommunityManagement.Checks integration suite for financial behavior. The designer checks are a compatibility preflight; they do not automate Visual Studio's editor itself.

Register defaults: Default accounts now includes asset purchases, supplier deposits paid, prepayments, accumulated depreciation, depreciation expense and write-offs/disposal expense. Starter accounts are added once to existing databases without changing customized assignments; occupied codes are preserved and a free code is chosen. A complete accounting reset restores these starter accounts and assignments. Register item types and opening schedule types select their defaults, while linked money-out entries retain their actual allocation account. Depreciation and write-off/disposal actions select their expense defaults; expense recognition remains an explicit choice because it depends on the prepaid service.

## Reports

Use the Reports sidebar section to generate Balance Sheet, Income Statement and Trial Balance from posted journal entries. Balance Sheet and Trial Balance use an as-of date; Income Statement uses an inclusive date range. Inactive accounts with balances remain included, and an option shows zero-balance accounts. Draft bills and draft adjustments are excluded until posted.

Balance Sheet includes cumulative asset/liability/equity balances and splits unclosed earnings into prior years and the selected calendar year. Accumulated depreciation reduces assets. Trial Balance shows cumulative net debit/credit balances. Income Statement includes posted income and expenses, including unpaid maintenance charges, unpaid supplier bills and noncash adjustments. Reports do not create annual closing journals or change balances.

Click Generate after changing filters. Export CSV opens in Excel, and Print / preview supports printing through installed printers (including Microsoft Print to PDF where available). Export and print use the generated report and are disabled when its filters change.
